# Engine status

Facts as of 2026-08-15. This document states what exists in the repository today and the rules governing changes to the frozen packages. It contains no roadmap, priorities, or aspirational language. For the generated inventory (debug_report keys, wired calculators, ABC-stub file table, live test counts), see `docs/status_generated.md` — that file is regenerated by `tools/gen_status.py` and CI fails if it's stale; nothing in it is hand-maintained, so it is not duplicated here.

## Frozen: engine/tracking, engine/preprocessing, engine/biomechanics

As of 2026-08-01, `engine/tracking/`, `engine/preprocessing/`, and `engine/biomechanics/` are feature-frozen. This followed a validation-and-remediation pass that fixed two FAIL-level bugs: weight-transfer numerical instability near a narrow stance, and `MissedFramePersistence` never decaying confidence on held-over landmarks.

### Freeze rules

- No modification to mathematical algorithms in the frozen packages unless correcting a validated bug (reproduced via a hand-computed synthetic case and/or real-video evidence).
- Any change affecting existing numerical outputs (angle values, velocities, confidence numbers, etc.) requires a before/after regression report and explicit approval before landing.
- New work in these packages must be additive (new metric files/measurement types), not edits to existing calculators' formulas.
- Measurement dataclasses in `engine/types/biomechanics.py` are backward-compatible: additive fields only, no renames/removals, no field-order changes.
- `engine/pipelines/` is explicitly **not** covered by this freeze.

### Known limitations (unfixed at freeze time, not new findings)

- `CenterOfMassCalculator` (`engine/biomechanics/posture/center_of_mass.py`) uses the geometric midpoint for segment centers instead of Winter's per-segment anthropometric fraction.
- Pelvis/shoulder rotation (heading angles, ±180° wraparound) are not safe to differentiate into a velocity — no angle-unwrapping step exists.
- No multi-person tracking support.

### Weight-transfer coverage finding (2026-08-15) — scoped to one clip, not a general claim

On one specific real forehand clip (941 frames, "Serious Squash..." sample video), `weight_transfer` came back valid on 85.55% of frames, versus 100% on one specific real backhand clip (293 frames, `sample_backhand2.mp4`). Root cause on that forehand clip, confirmed by inspecting the underlying landmark data directly: `left_ankle` visibility/presence repeatedly dropping below the 0.5 threshold during the swing, not narrow stance and not a calculator bug. This is **not** established as "forehand posture is less reliable than backhand" in general — it is one data point from one clip of one player from one camera angle. Histogram-correlation cut detection over the same clip found no scene cuts (minimum consecutive-frame correlation 0.9744 across all 941 frames), so the coverage gap is not attributable to edited/cut footage in this clip. Generalizing beyond this one clip would require testing more forehand footage across different players, camera angles, and swing styles — that testing has not been done.

## engine/phases

Not covered by the freeze above. `engine/phases/contact_detection.py` (two independent contact rules: `peak_speed`, `deceleration_onset`, plus multi-swing windowing via `segment_swing_windows`) and `engine/phases/phase_detector.py` (`KinematicPhaseDetector`, implementing the existing `PhaseDetector.detect()` ABC) are implemented and evaluated against real labelled clips via `tools/eval_phases.py` — see that tool's output for current accuracy numbers, not reproduced here since they change as `labels/` grows. Not validated for production use.

### Known limitations

- **Tight rally pacing under-segments swing windows.** `SWING_MIN_REST_GAP_FRAMES` (currently 15) can't be lowered to fix this: on `sample_backhand3.mp4`, real inter-swing rest periods measure 5.1–7.0% of the clip's peak speed at their deepest point, and a genuine single-swing mid-swing dip on `sample_forehand1.mp4` measures 6.4–6.9% — the same depth range. Duration doesn't separate them either (backhand3's real rests are as short as 5 frames; forehand1's genuine internal dip is 12 frames — the ranges overlap). No single threshold, on either signal, splits the former without also splitting the latter. Confirmed 2026-08-27: 3 of `sample_backhand3.mp4`'s real swings each get merged with a neighboring swing into one detection window, and stay merged.
- **`forward_swing`'s boundary is unreliable by an amount that varies per swing, with no identified predictor.** `_segment_one_swing` derives it from a shoulder-rotation-extremum search over `[backswing_start, contact_frame]`, falling back to the speed-minimum in that range when rotation data is invalid. Reviewed against 6 real matched swings (2026-08-28): the extremum landed within a few frames of the human-confirmed boundary in 2/6 and was 6–16 frames early in the other 4. Neither a fixed offset nor a span-proportional one reconciles both groups: a flat +10.5-frame correction (the median of the 4 wrong cases) brings those to within 2.5–5.5 frames but pushes the 2 already-correct cases to −10.5 frames off; the needed-correction-to-span ratio looked consistent (0.24–0.30) within the 4 wrong cases alone but breaks once the 2 correct cases are included (one has a mid-range span and needs zero correction). Applying speed-minimum directly (not just as the fallback) was also tested and is worse, not better: it matched the extremum's accuracy on only 1/6 candidates and was substantially worse on the two largest-error cases (35 vs. 16 frames error, 28 vs. 13 frames error). No signal tried so far — extremum, speed-minimum, fixed offset, span-proportional offset — predicts which swings will be off or by how much. Needs a different detection approach, not a correction layered on either existing signal.
- **A swing whose source footage starts already mid-motion has no usable prep or windowing signal.** Confirmed on `sample_backhand1.mp4`'s contact=3 (2026-08-29) by inspecting the raw frames directly: the player is already lunging with the racket arm cocked and the ball already in flight at frame 0 — no backswing or rest period is captured before this contact at all. Wrist speed is correspondingly elevated (roughly 400–2600, never near zero) from frame 1 onward, so the motion never sustains its own clean above-threshold stretch long enough to register as a window under any `SWING_MIN_WINDOW_MS` value tried; contact=3 has never been detected. Same family as the unconditional `prep = range_start` limitation above — caused by the source footage itself starting mid-swing, not a detector bug, and not fixable by threshold tuning.
- **Duration alone cannot separate real swings from incidental motion — proven, not suspected, and true in every unit tested.** Measured across all 6 labelled clips (2026-08-29): real swing-window durations range 317ms–4800ms (14–144 frames depending on the clip's own rate); surviving incidental-window durations range 733ms–833ms (22–25 frames) — these ranges overlap (the shortest real window, 317ms/14 frames on `sample_forehand1.mp4`, is shorter than the longest incidental one). No single `SWING_MIN_WINDOW_MS`/`SWING_MIN_WINDOW_FRAMES` threshold, in frames or in milliseconds, can keep every real window and drop every incidental one — the corpus's own numbers make that combination impossible, not just untried. `SWING_MIN_WINDOW_MS = 250` (see above) was chosen to keep every currently-labelled real swing, at the cost of re-admitting a small, quantified number of incidental windows that a stricter threshold would have filtered (Part 0 regression report, 2026-08-29) — a deliberate trade-off, not a fix. The identified real fix — duration *and* prominence (requiring the window to also contain a speed peak above some fraction of the clip's own peak, decoupling "how long" from "how noticeable") — has not been built; it's a design change beyond a threshold-tuning pass.
- **Filtering a short window widens its surviving neighbor's phase-search range, and measurably costs `follow_through` accuracy.** Raising `SWING_MIN_WINDOW_FRAMES` from 5 to 13 (see above) removed 13 windows across 5 of 6 labelled clips; each removal widens the range its surviving neighbor searches for prep/follow_through/recovery boundaries by 12–112 frames (the range-midpoint construction now reaches past where the removed window used to bound it), and the removed window's own speed peak reliably reappears as a secondary local maximum inside the newly-added range in nearly every case — confirmed directly on `sample_forehand2.mp4`'s swing 2 and generalized across all 6 clips (2026-08-29). Cross-referenced against the 35-item human phase review (2026-08-29): `follow_through` accuracy is 0/3 correct on swings whose range was widened vs. 4/4 correct on swings whose range wasn't — a clean split, though on a very small sample. `recovery` shows no comparable split at this sample size (2/5 vs. 1/3) — not established as affected the same way. This is a real, quantified cost of the `SWING_MIN_WINDOW_FRAMES` fix above, not a hypothesis; not addressed by any change made so far.

## Roadmap position

The frozen tracking/preprocessing/biomechanics packages plus `ShotPipeline` (`engine/pipelines/shots/shot_pipeline.py`, replacing the earlier separate ForehandPipeline/BackhandPipeline) are the only implemented, tested, production-track layers. `engine/scoring`, `engine/feedback`, `engine/calibration`, `engine/persistence`, and `engine/session` remain unimplemented interface/dataclass stubs (see `docs/status_generated.md` for the current file-by-file inventory) — no scoring or coaching feedback exists yet. `engine/phases` has contact-detection and phase-segmentation logic implemented and under active evaluation (see the `engine/phases` section above), but is not validated for production use. Nothing past this point (what gets built next, in what order) is recorded here by design: planning lives in `docs/ROADMAP.md`, generated by `tools/gen_roadmap.py` from one node file per capability in `docs/roadmap/` (status, dependencies, validation bar, evidence), with its own CI freshness check.
