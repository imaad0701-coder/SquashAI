# Court calibration evidence (2026-10-02)

Generated by `python tools/validate_court_calibration.py`. The numbers are in `report.json` and the overlay images are `<clip>_overlay.jpg`. Regenerate after changing any `calibrations/*.json` file or `engine/calibration/`.

**Verdict: neither calibration can be trusted across its clip.** Each is valid only near its calibration frame, because **the camera moves during both clips**. Positions near the camera are also extrapolated beyond every clicked point.

## How the points were placed

- **Who placed them:** Claude, from 5–6× zoomed crops of one frame per clip, at line centres. They are not human clicks, and each point's `note` in `calibrations/<clip>.json` says so.
- **Why so few:** the footage is filmed from behind the back wall, so only the front half of the floor is in view, and the player covers the centre in every frame checked. Each clip ended up with exactly 5 usable points, the minimum for leave-one-out.
- **backhand3's front-right corner** is inferred from where the tin-board base ends. The base of the right wall isn't visible.

The two "not computable" rows are left out on purpose. Removing either point leaves three points on the right-wall line plus one other, which has no unique homography. An earlier version of the fit didn't catch this and printed a meaningless "105.9 m". It now refuses.

**Error depends strongly on where the point is on the court:**
- Points among the service-box lines are predicted to within 0.03–0.15 m.
- Front-wall corners, held out and predicted from points 5+ m away, are off by 0.5–1.75 m.

With only 5 points, three explanations can't be told apart:
- lens distortion, which a phone's wide lens would cause and which grows towards the image edges
- the leverage of extrapolating far from the other points
- a misplaced front-wall point, with backhand3's inferred right corner the prime suspect

**What would separate them:** more points spread across the floor on one frame, or a checkpoint at a known position that isn't used in the fit. This footage doesn't give either. Until that is available, it's undetermined whether lens-distortion correction is needed. These numbers neither rule it out nor confirm it.

- **sample_backhand3:**
  - On frame 286, the left box, short line and left-wall base line up by eye. The right half can't be checked, because the player and a glass bracket hide it.
  - On frames 0, 198 and 395, the real box sits about 90 px (full resolution) from the projection. **This camera pans, probably handheld.**

## Foot positions

Feet are each foot's toe landmark mapped to court metres, when the landmark passes the visibility gate.

- **On the calibration frames** the positions look plausible against the overlays:
  - backhand3 frame 286: left foot (1.74, 8.07) m, right foot (1.70, 7.61) m, just behind the left box's back corner, as in the image.
  - forehand1 frame 0: left foot (4.75, 7.63) m, right foot (5.02, 8.32) m.
- **Every one of these is flagged extrapolated**, because the feet are nearer the camera than any clicked point.
- **The whole-clip numbers in `report.json` aren't meaningful,** because the camera moves. They count as "inside court bounds", but that is not validation.

## What this means for the roadmap

A single static calibration per clip doesn't work for this corpus. The next step is one of these:
- **Per-frame tracking of the calibration:** re-estimate the homography as the camera moves, for example by registering each frame to the calibration frame, or by tracking the clicked lines.
- **Tripod footage:** require a fixed camera for this feature, and check that requirement automatically at upload.

Either way, positions behind the service boxes, which is where players mostly stand in this footage, stay extrapolated unless points nearer the camera can be clicked.
