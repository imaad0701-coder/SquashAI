# Camera-motion evidence (2026-10-02)

## 1. Pre-flight camera-motion check: sanity test

Generated by `python tools/survey_camera_motion.py`, written to `preflight_survey.json`.

The check (`measure_camera_motion` in `demo/backend/preflight.py`) works like this:
- Corner features are tracked on frames sampled every 100 ms, a fixed real-time interval.
- A RANSAC similarity transform is fitted at each step, so features on the player are rejected.
- The steps are composed into drift relative to frame 0, expressed as a percentage of the frame diagonal.

| Clip | Drift | Rotation | Zoom | Check | Confirmed by eye |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sample_backhand3 | 7.1% | 1.2° | 2.0% | **warn** | moving (court overlay drifts ~90 px) |
| sample_forehand1 | 6.8% | 1.1° | 1.2% | **warn** | moving (court overlay drifts by mid-clip) |
| sample_forehand3 | 9.9% | 2.8° | 4.6% | **warn** | moving (frame difference shows court lines doubled) |
| sample_backhand2 | 1.1% | 0.6° | 0.7% | pass | static (frame difference shows only the player) |
| sample_backhand (fixture) | 0.6% | 0.1° | 0.3% | pass | not checked |
| sample_forehand2 | 0.4% | 0.2° | 0.3% | pass | static |
| sample_backhand1 | 0.2% | 0.0° | 0.3% | pass | not checked |

**Result:**
- Both calibrated clips are clearly flagged, which matches what the court overlays showed independently.
- The check also flagged forehand3, and that was confirmed by eye.
- The clips confirmed static measure 0.4–1.1%. The 1.1% on backhand2 is false drift on a close-up where the player fills the frame, so about 1% is this check's noise floor.

The warning line is set at **3%, provisionally**, in the gap between 1.1% and 6.8%. It comes from 7 clips and is not an established threshold. No clip had tracking gaps.

## 2. Research experiment: carrying a calibration through camera motion

Generated by `tools/camera_motion_experiment.py` (`track`, `crops`, `report`). The roadmap node is `docs/roadmap/camera-motion-tracking.md`.

**Method:**
- **Tracking:** features are taken from the floor only, inside the calibration's floor polygon, which is warped along with the camera. The player is masked out using their pose landmarks. Optical flow runs frame to frame at full rate, with a RANSAC homography fitted at each step. Floor points lie on one plane, so a homography is exact for the floor even when the camera translates.
- **Ground truth:** the calibration points' true positions at 7 times through the clip were read **independently** from 4× crops (`crops/`, `sample_forehand1_ground_truth.json`, reading precision about ±1.5 px). Points the player hid at a given time were left out, not guessed.

### Error at the calibration points vs time since the calibration frame (sample_forehand1, 720×1280)

| Time | Tracked error (range across visible points) | Static calibration, no tracking (range) |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0 s | 1.4–3.8 px | 13.8–14.8 px |
| 3.0 s | 1.8–3.8 px | 13.5–13.8 px |
| 5.0 s | 2.1–6.4 px | 63.7–65.9 px |
| 7.0 s | 7.1–8.9 px | 51.2–56.3 px |
| 9.0 s | 7.7–11.4 px | 48.4–55.9 px |
| 11.0 s | 11.3–22.7 px | 61.9–72.8 px |
| 13.2 s | 11.9–20.3 px | 66.7–75.8 px |

The per-point numbers are in `sample_forehand1_errors.json`, and the plot is `sample_forehand1_error_vs_time.png`.

**What it shows:**
- Tracking cuts the error **4–8×** compared with using the frame-0 calibration as it is.
- Tracked error **grows steadily, by roughly 1–1.5 px per second of clip**, from about 2–4 px in the first 3 s to 12–20 px by 13 s. It doesn't blow up, but it doesn't level off either. This is the expected signature of small per-step errors accumulating along a chain of composed homographies, over 795 steps here.
- For scale: the calibration's own leave-one-out error at these points was 3–6 px. Tracking stays inside that for about the first 3–5 s, and is about 2–4× worse by the end of a 13 s clip. Near the service boxes, 15 px is roughly 0.1–0.15 m.

### Tracking gaps: not observed on this footage

The request was to confirm whether frames with no trackable features happen on real footage. **In what was tested, they didn't:**
- **sample_forehand1,** 795 steps over 13.3 s: no gaps. The weakest step still had 215 floor inliers.
- **sample_backhand3,** forward from its calibration frame for 109 steps (3.6 s): no gaps. The weakest step kept 108 inliers, at about 2.9 s with the player close.

Both courts have wood-grain floors, which give plenty of texture. **Not tested:** plain or glossy floors, players blocking most of the floor, motion blur from fast pans, and tracking *backwards* from the calibration frame. Gaps are most likely in exactly those cases. The tracker does handle a gap: it keeps the last known transform and flags every later frame as post-gap, because there's nothing to re-anchor to without re-calibrating. That path never ran on real data, so what a gap costs is **still unmeasured**.

A high inlier count also doesn't prove a correct lock. RANSAC could in principle lock onto a consistent but wrong set of points. The ground-truth errors above are the actual check on correctness, and they only exist for forehand1.

- **Backward reached frame 0 with no gap.** The fewest inliers at any step was 68, lower than the forward minimum of 108.
- **Backward behaves like forward.** As a percentage of the frame diagonal:
  - backhand3 backward: 0.1–0.36% up to 3.2 s, at most 0.50% at 4.9 s, and 0.4–0.8% at 6.8–8.5 s.

  - The front-right corner is the calibration's own inferred point, so its readings are the least certain.
  - Points hidden by the player were left out.

## 4. The practical split, implemented

The code is `engine/calibration/court_capabilities.py`, and the real-clip output is `court_capabilities.json` (from `tools/court_capability_report.py`).

- **Per swing:** available within **5 s** of the anchor in either direction, with the elapsed time and a **combined uncertainty**. The horizon and the tracking envelope (0.3% + 0.05% per second of the frame diagonal) are provisional, set from the readings above: every reading within 5 s falls inside the envelope.

### Combined uncertainty (reported ±)

The reported ± is the sum of two parts, computed for each foot; the less certain foot's figure is reported:
- **Calibration error:** the calibration's leave-one-out errors (section 2 of `docs/evidence/court_calibration/README.md`), interpolated at the foot's court position by inverse-distance weighting.
- **Tracking error:** the pixel envelope above, converted to metres at the foot's location.

The two are added straight, not in quadrature, to stay conservative. If a calibration has fewer than 5 points, leave-one-out can't run and the ± is reported as **unknown**, never as the tracking part alone.

| Clip | Swing (contact frame) | Elapsed from anchor | **± combined** | Calibration part | Tracking part |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| backhand3 | 1 (f191) | 3.17 s backward | **0.14 m** | 0.10 m | 0.04 m |
| backhand3 | 2 (f342) | 1.87 s forward | **0.13 m** | 0.10 m | 0.03 m |
| backhand3 | 3 (f394) | 3.60 s forward | **0.13 m** | 0.09 m | 0.04 m |
| forehand1 | 0 (f34) | 0.57 s forward | **0.14 m** | 0.09 m | 0.04 m |
| forehand1 | 1 (f145) | 2.42 s forward | **0.16 m** | 0.09 m | 0.07 m |
| forehand1 | 2 (f255) | 4.26 s forward | **0.17 m** | 0.09 m | 0.08 m |

Unavailable: backhand3 swing 0 (8.5 s from the anchor) and forehand1 swings 3–5 (8.0–11.8 s).

**The combined ± is roughly 3× the tracking-only figure reported before** (0.03–0.08 m), and the calibration part dominates.

### Calibration error at reference spots: where positions would be much less precise

No real foot position in these clips is near a corner, because the players stand behind the service boxes. The calibration's own error is very uneven across the court, though:

| Spot | backhand3 | forehand1 |
|---|---|---|
| front-left corner area | **0.59 m** | 0.29 m* |
| front-right corner area | **1.69 m** | **0.50 m** |
| T | 0.14 m | 0.14 m* |
| behind left service box | 0.09 m | 0.15 m* |
| behind right service box | 0.26 m* | 0.10 m |
| back-centre | 0.15 m* | 0.12 m* |

A position near a front corner on backhand3 would carry an uncertainty of over 1.7 m, more than ten times that of the same clip's positions behind the left box.

\* **These estimates are too low to trust.** The nearest clicked point is 2.6–5.9 m away. For example, forehand1 has no clicked point on the left side of the court at all. Inverse-distance interpolation just averages the nearest points' errors, so far from every clicked point it **understates** the error. Leave-one-out itself shows error growing with distance from the other points: box points held out about 1.6 m from their neighbours were off by 0.03–0.15 m, and front corners held out more than 5 m away were off by 0.5–1.75 m. The output shows each estimate's nearest-point distance so this stays visible. The real foot positions above sit 1.0–1.3 m from their nearest clicked point, comparable to the spacing at which leave-one-out was measured.

- **Whole-clip movement trail:** unavailable on both clips (moving camera), with that reason. It's only offered when the camera-motion check classifies the camera as static.

## Conclusion

Tractable for short clips, with drift that is understood but unsolved:
- Floor tracking keeps a single calibration usable, within about 4 px, for **about 3 s** on this clip. It degrades gradually after that, with no sudden failure.
- Clips longer than a few seconds need **drift control**: periodically re-anchoring to the court lines, or matching each frame back to the calibration frame instead of chaining frame to frame.
- These numbers come from **one clip with one set of ground truth**, so they are not a general result.
